What is this Talk About?

Almost any imaginable computational problem has been successfully* addressed using AI techniques.

but...

  • Reproducing published results is in general not feasible.
  • AI solutions usually fail miserably when translated to real-life scenarios.

why? Many reasons, but today we will focus on a highly disctinctive one:

Contrary to most disciplines, in AI methodological errors often lead to apparently better results.

When Bugs Become Friends

The Equation of Machine Learning

$$ \begin{equation*} \theta^* = \argmin_{\theta} \mathcal{L}(\theta; \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{y}) \end{equation*} $$

where

  • $\theta$ is the set of parameters defining a model.
  • $\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{y}$ are training data with inputs ($\mathbf{X}$) and known outputs ($\mathbf{y}$).
  • $\mathcal{L}$ is a loss function measuring the quality of the model.

Training a Model through Gradient Descent

$$ \begin{equation*} \theta_{n+1} = \theta_n - \alpha \nabla_{\theta_n} \mathcal{L}(\theta_n; \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{y}) \end{equation*} $$
• Basic Example
$$ \begin{align*} \mathbf{y} &= a\cdot \mathbf{X} + b + \epsilon\\ \epsilon &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.1)\\ \theta &= \{a=0.3, b=0.8\} \end{align*} $$
• Training Setup
$$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{L}(\theta_n; \mathbf{X}, \mathbf{y}) &:= MSE(a_n\cdot \mathbf{X} + b_n, \mathbf{y})\\ \theta_0 &= \{a_0 = 0.0, b_0=0.0\}\\ \alpha &= 0.001 \end{align*} $$

The Pitfalls of Real World, and ADAM as "Solution"

Basic Gradient Descent

$$ \begin{equation*} \theta_{n+1} = \theta_n - \alpha \nabla_{\theta_n} \mathcal{L} \end{equation*} $$

ADAM: Adaptive Moment Estimation

$$ \begin{align*} \theta_{n+1} &= \theta_n - \frac{\alpha~\textcolor{green}{m_{n+1}}}{\sqrt{\textcolor{green}{v_{n+1}}} + \epsilon}\\ \end{align*} $$
$$ m_{n+1} = \beta_1 m_n + (1-\beta_1) \nabla_{\theta_n} \mathcal{L};\quad v_{n+1} = \beta_2 v_n + (1-\beta_2) (\nabla_{\theta_n} \mathcal{L})^2 $$

How "well" do they Learn?

  • Greedy search: Always following min gradient.
  • Extreme flexibility: Up to trillions of parameters.
  • Any shortcut in the model, loss function or data will be exploited!

How Well does DL Reproduce Expert Knowledge?

Experiment:
Given a sequence of RR intervals $[RR_1, \ldots, $$_n]$ in a 30-second window, check if:
$$ \exists i \in \{1, \ldots, n-2\} ~|~ min(RR_i, RR_{i+1}, RR_{i+2}) > 1000ms ~\text{OR}~\\ \hspace{6.5em} max(RR_i, RR_{i+1}, RR_{i+2}) < 500ms $$
  • Dataset: Public MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database, MLII Lead
    • 37 records for training, 9 for testing.
    • Segments of 30 seconds duration, with different overlapping for assessing the relevance of the dataset size.
    • Not so imbalanced dataset: 75% negatives, 25% positives.
  • Model: State-of-the-art network achieving good performance in the Physionet Challenge.
Perfect class separability (easy problem)! But...
  • Requires signal abstraction (Heart beat detection)
  • Requires distance calculations (RRs)
  • Requires counting up to 3.
  • Requires a logical "OR" operation.

Concluding Remarks

  1. Beware of Goodhart's law: Any metric that becomes a target ceases to be a good metric.
  2. Models are cheaters: Usually they don't do what you think they do, they just pretend to do it.
  3. If you torture the data long enough, it will confess to anything.
